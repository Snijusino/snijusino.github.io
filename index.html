UNTOLD STORY - СНИДЖУ.АШНА

I still remember being in first grade. Most kids that age worry about toys or games, but my foundation was already cracking. It was midnight when it happened. My parents were screaming over something small, something meaningless, and then their anger just… shifted. It landed on me. I was beaten so badly that I tried to scream for help, but nothing came out. It felt like something was physically blocking my throat. I couldn’t speak. I could only cry.

That was the first time I felt that "lock." It stayed with me. Every time I was bullied at school or let down by the people I trusted, that same feeling would return. My mouth would just shut. Even when I was right, even when I had the truth, I stayed silent. When I finally tried to tell my family how much I was hurting, they just called it "drama." They told me I was weak. So, I stopped trying. I built a wall and started living behind a mask, acting like someone else while the real me felt like a ghost in a place that didn't exist anymore.

The Drift

By 8th grade, I was checked out. Everything felt gray and lifeless, so I started smoking and using tobacco—anything to get a quick hit of energy or a moment of peace. The world had already decided I was a "bad kid," so I stopped fighting it. I just leaned into the title.

Then the 2019 pandemic hit. I got my first phone for online classes, but while everyone else was focused on school, I found a way out. I started learning how to build things online. I started writing code and exploring how digital systems worked. For the first time, I felt like I was actually good at something. I wasn't the "weak" kid there; I was someone who understood the architecture of a whole different world. But as much as I grew online, the real world was waiting to pull me back down.

Ashna

When we finally went back to school, I remember sitting alone at my desk, feeling that cold breeze from the window. I looked up, and there she was: Ashna. Her eyes completely caught me off guard. The first time she asked my name, I was so overwhelmed I actually ran away. I had to go back the next day just to say it.

We became best friends. I loved her, but I was terrified that if I said anything, I’d lose the only good thing in my life. When I finally did tell her how I felt, things went south—not because of her, but because of others. A group of guys cornered me and beat me for even approaching her. That old feeling returned. My throat locked. I couldn't defend myself. Then I was told she was the one who wanted them to hurt me. I believed the lie. I spent months in pain, smoking more and more just to numb the heartbreak.

Eventually, I found out a "friend" had set us both up with lies. We reconnected, and on October 20th, she told me she had loved me from the start. That was the only high I ever needed. She wasn't just my girlfriend; she was my home.

The Crash

The last two years felt like a constant struggle. I tried to quit everything for her after 10th grade, but the withdrawals made me feel like a different person. I went numb. My body was failing, but I hid it because I wanted to be there for her. I waited at the bus stand every single day just to see her face.

But then, everything started falling apart. My grandmother passed away—the one person who truly gave me a home when things were at their worst. Then more friends betrayed me. I kept telling myself it was okay because I still had her.

Then came January 2026. She told me she needed a break. On January 24th, at that same bus stand, she ended it. That "stuck" feeling came back, but this time it wasn't a physical block—it was pure, heavy stress. I lost myself. I sat for my board exams but didn't care. I couldn't sleep. Every time I closed my eyes, the memories just played over and over again.

My Final Entry

I look in the mirror now and I don’t recognize the person looking back. I feel like I’ve failed as a son, a brother, and a boyfriend. People think I’m doing okay because I’ve become an expert at faking a smile, but I’m struggling with a pain that’s heavier than any habit I ever had.

I loved her more than anything in this universe. I still do. She is my home, even if the doors are locked now. If I ever disappear, it’s not just because of a breakup; it’s the weight of every trauma, every betrayal, and every silent moment I’ve had to carry since I was a child. I tried to be better. I really did. I’m sorry. I loved you more than anything else. I still love you. My wife...
